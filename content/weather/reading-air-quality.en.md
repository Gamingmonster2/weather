# Understanding air quality and dust in Libya

Air quality across Libya is shaped by desert dust, coastal humidity, traffic, and seasonal winds. weather.com.ly shows the European AQI alongside PM2.5, PM10 and dust estimates from Open-Meteo so you can judge conditions at a glance.

## The numbers that matter

- **European AQI** — an index from 0 upward where lower is better. Values under 20 are generally good; above 60 sensitive groups may feel effects.
- **PM2.5** — fine particles small enough to enter deep into the lungs. Dust events and combustion are common sources.
- **PM10** — larger particles including wind-blown sand and dust.
- **Dust** — a direct estimate useful during ghibli (hot southerly) episodes.

## Ghibli winds and dust storms

In spring and autumn, strong southerly ghibli winds can lift Saharan dust and carry it north to the coast. Visibility can drop sharply, temperatures spike, and AQI can degrade within hours. If the forecast shows rising dust with strengthening south winds, plan outdoor activity accordingly.

## Coastal vs inland patterns

Coastal cities often benefit from sea breezes that disperse pollutants, while inland basins can trap dust under stable air. After rain, particulate levels usually fall — one reason the rain and air views complement each other across the two sites.

## Practical tips

- On high-dust days, limit strenuous outdoor exercise and keep windows closed.
- Sensitive groups should watch PM2.5 specifically, not just the headline AQI.
- Check the hourly trend: a short spike differs from a day-long event.
