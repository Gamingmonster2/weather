# Flash floods and wadis: staying safe in Libya

In Libya, some of the most dangerous flooding happens far from the coast and sometimes far from where the rain actually fell. Dry valleys (*wadis*) can turn into fast-moving torrents within minutes when intense showers hit upstream catchments.

## Why wadis flood so quickly

- **Hard, dry ground** — after long dry spells, soil and rock absorb little water at first, so runoff concentrates rapidly.
- **Steep catchments** — mountain and plateau terrain funnels water into narrow channels.
- **Localized downpours** — a short, intense convective burst can deliver large totals over a small area while nearby places stay dry.
- **Upstream rainfall** — clear skies above you do not guarantee a safe wadi if heavy rain fell higher in the basin.

## Warning signs to take seriously

- A sudden rise in water level, noise, or debris moving downstream.
- Rapidly darkening skies over nearby high ground or repeated thunder.
- Radar or forecast showing intense cells or repeated bands over the same catchment.
- Road crossings that look shallow but are flowing fast — moving water can sweep vehicles away.

## Practical safety steps

1. **Avoid driving through flooded crossings.** Depth is hard to judge and the road surface may be damaged underneath.
2. **Stay out of wadis during and after storms.** Water can arrive as a surge even if local rain has stopped.
3. **Plan routes with terrain in mind.** Low crossings and underpasses are the first to become hazardous.
4. **Check conditions before travel.** Combine the live radar, hourly probability, and accumulation on [Rain.ly home](/) and your [city page](/cities.html) to understand timing and intensity.
