# Rain seasons across Libya's coast, mountains and desert

Libya spans Mediterranean shoreline, uplifted highlands and the Sahara — so "rainy season" means very different things depending on where you stand.

## The Mediterranean coast

From Tripoli to Benghazi, most rain falls between **November and March** when mid-latitude cyclones track across the central Mediterranean. Totals are modest but highly variable year to year; a single slow-moving cut-off low can deliver a month's worth of rain in a day.

**What to watch:** onshore flow against coastal hills, rapid pressure falls, and training thunderstorm bands.

## Jabal al-Akhdar and the western mountains

Al Bayda, Shahat and Gharyan sit higher and cooler. Orographic lift squeezes extra moisture from moist air masses, making the Green Mountain the wettest part of the country. Fog, low cloud and gusty ridge winds are common in winter.

**What to watch:** persistent low cloud that keeps temperatures suppressed, and localized heavy rain in wadis below steep terrain.

## Fezzan and the oases

Sabha, Ubari, Ghat and Kufra are hyper-arid. Annual rainfall is sparse and erratic, often arriving as brief, intense convective bursts that can trigger flash flooding in dry valleys far from where the rain fell.

**What to watch:** summer convection building over high ground to the south, and any forecast showing even small accumulations — in the desert, a little rain goes a long way.
