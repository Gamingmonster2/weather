# Desert dust... when does the sky turn hazy?

Sometimes you wake up and the sky is yellow.

No rain.
No clouds.
Just dust in the air.

![Sky above the desert | Sand dunes under storm clouds](/assets/articles/desert-storm-sky.jpg)

## Where does it come from?

Strong wind lifts very fine sand from the ground.

The sand rises fast.
It travels hundreds of kilometres.
Then it settles over cities.

![A whirl over the sand | Aerial view of a desert whirlwind](/assets/articles/desert-whirlwind.jpg)

## What happens to visibility?

The more dust in the air, the less you can see.

Far things disappear.
Sunlight feels weak.
The air may even feel hotter than it is.

## Is this rain?

No.

Dust is not rain.
The rain radar does not see it well.

So do not wait for a drop.
Wait for the wind to calm down.

![Dunes in the Sahara | Sahara sand dunes at sunset](/assets/articles/sahara-dunes.jpg)

## Small tips

- Close the windows when the dust gets strong.
- Drive slowly and switch on your lights.
- Check the [Libya rain map](/guides/live-rain-map-libya.html) before travelling.
- Read the [radar guide](/guides/reading-rain-radar.html) to tell dust from rain.

## When does it end?

When the wind calms, the dust falls.

Colours come back.
The sky clears.
In a day or two, you forget it was ever yellow.
