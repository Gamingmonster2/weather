# Live rain map of Libya: how to read it and use it

When people search for a **rain map of Libya** or **خريطة الأمطار ليبيا**, they usually want one thing: to know whether rain is coming, where it is now, and how strong it will be. The live map on Rain.ly puts the official OpenStreetMap basemap, a RainViewer precipitation layer and your selected location in a single view. This guide explains what each part means.

## What you are looking at

The map has two layers stacked on top of each other:

- **The basemap** — streets, coastlines and place names from OpenStreetMap. Use it for orientation, not for weather.
- **The precipitation layer** — coloured areas from the RainViewer radar network, updated every few minutes. This is the part that shows where rain is actually falling.

Your location marker and the selected point marker are drawn above both layers. Tap anywhere on the map to move the selected point and load a forecast for that exact coordinate.

## Reading the intensity colours

Radar colours describe how much water the radar sees falling, usually in millimetres per hour:

- **Light blue / green** — light rain or drizzle. Little impact on driving.
- **Yellow / orange** — moderate rain. Standing water can form on roads.
- **Red / purple** — heavy rain and possibly thunderstorms. Avoid wadis and low-lying roads.

Colour is more useful than the weather icon alone: a "showers" icon can hide a short but intense burst that shows clearly on radar.

## Movement and timing

Radar is a sequence of frames, not a single picture. Watch two or three frames in a row:

1. Note the **direction** the coloured band is moving.
2. Estimate how many minutes until it reaches your city or selected point.
3. Check whether the band is **growing** (strengthening) or fading.

This short-range look-ahead is often called *nowcasting*, and it is most reliable for the next 30–60 minutes. Beyond that, use the [hourly forecast](/guides/hourly-rain-forecast-libya.html) instead.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing the basemap with the radar.** Grey roads and blue sea are not weather data.
- **Reading a single frame.** A gap between radar scans can make rain appear or disappear.
- **Assuming radar equals ground truth.** Radar can overestimate in intense convective storms and miss shallow drizzle.
